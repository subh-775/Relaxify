# Data & Storage

Where your things are kept. You do not need this page to use the app.

## Everything runs on your phone

Searching, ranking, starting a stream and downloading all happen on your phone. There is no Relaxify server, so there is no account. What leaves your phone is the usage statistics below, and, only while you are in a [Jam](/guide/jam), the Jam's own state.

The engine is a small server **inside the app**. It cannot be reached from your network. It is locked with a password the app creates fresh every launch and never shares, because other apps on the same phone could otherwise talk to it.

Web pages in your browser cannot read its replies. The password protects everything that touches your library.

## What is saved, and where

| What | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Playlists, likes, history, settings | The app's storage on your phone |
| Downloaded songs | Your own music folder |
| Lyrics, covers, temporary files | Cache. **Settings → Clear cached data** removes them, and **Clear cache automatically** does once it passes your limit |

Uninstalling deletes the first row. Downloads stay, because they are in a folder you chose. See [Downloads](/guide/downloads).

## Usage statistics

To help improve the app, it sends usage statistics to Google (Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics):

- songs played and how long each was listened to, searches, likes, playlists and downloads
- settings you change, errors, crashes and playback problems
- the app version, the phone model and your approximate location (country and city, worked out from your internet address)

They are linked to an anonymous ID for your phone, never to your name, account or contacts. The app never asks for your location. **Recap** is worked out on your phone from your own history and sends nothing.

## Jam

During a [Jam](/guide/jam), the song, where it is, playing or paused, the shared queue and the names people enter are kept in a Firebase database so the phones can follow each other. A Jam can only be read by someone who has its code, and it is deleted when it ends (at the latest six hours after it started). No audio passes through it.

## Audio effects

The equalizer and Normalize volume are Android's own effects, attached to the sound that is playing. That is why they need a song playing, and why the equalizer shows how many bands your phone has.

## Playing in the background

Android needs a **notification** for music to keep playing after you leave the app. That is why the app asks for notification permission the first time you play a song.

Swiping the app away from Recents stops the music and removes the notification.
